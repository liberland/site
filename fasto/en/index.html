Why it matters

Why formaldehyde is toxic

Formaldehyde is part of the urea-formaldehyde resins used to bond chipboard and other composite boards. It is gradually released from them into the air as a gas – most of all from new furniture and in warm or humid rooms. Its effect on health depends on the concentration in the air and how long you are exposed.

Short-term effects

At higher indoor concentrations

  • Irritated mucous membranes: burning, stinging and watering eyes, irritated nose and throat.
  • Breathing problems: coughing, shortness of breath, wheezing or a tight chest.
  • Skin reactions: allergic dermatitis and rashes in more sensitive people.
  • Neurological symptoms: headaches, nausea and unusual tiredness.
Long-term effects

With prolonged breathing

  • Airways: even lower concentrations can lead to asthma or make its symptoms worse, especially in children.
  • Cancer: The WHO’s International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classifies formaldehyde as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1). Long-term exposure raises the risk of nasopharyngeal cancer and is associated with a higher risk of leukaemia. This mainly concerns long-term occupational exposure in industry, but at home it is still sensible to keep concentrations as low as possible.

How to lower the risk at home

  1. 01Ventilate

    Air out new chipboard furniture or flooring thoroughly for the first few weeks.

  2. 02Check emission classes

    Choose materials in emission class E1 (the European standard), or better, E0 or no added formaldehyde.

  3. 03Mind the climate

    Keep rooms on the cooler side with moderate humidity. Heat and humidity speed up the release considerably.

  4. 04Seal the surfaces

    Every edge of a chipboard panel, including hidden ones at the back of wardrobes, should be edged, laminated or lacquered.

Source of the classification: IARC Monographs, list of classifications.

The simplest way is not to bring formaldehyde home at all. That is why our fitted wardrobes contain no chipboard.

FAQ

What people ask us

Is chipboard furniture toxic?

Chipboard is bonded with resins that often contain formaldehyde, which can gradually be released into the air. How much depends on the board grade and the age of the furniture. We don’t use chipboard at all, so it is not a question with our furniture.

Why is formaldehyde harmful?

At higher concentrations it irritates the eyes, nose and airways and can cause headaches. Long-term inhalation is linked to asthma, and the WHO’s IARC classifies formaldehyde as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1).

What are your fitted wardrobes made of?

Solid wood and boards of documented composition, with glues and finishes that contain no added formaldehyde. Every material comes with a technical data sheet.

What exactly does “no added formaldehyde” mean?

That no formaldehyde was added to the board, glue or finish during manufacture. Wood itself contains natural traces, so we don’t promise “zero emissions”; we document the specific materials instead.

Where do you build zomes?

In Czechia, in Croatia and in Liberland. Tell us where you want to build and we will arrange a site visit.
